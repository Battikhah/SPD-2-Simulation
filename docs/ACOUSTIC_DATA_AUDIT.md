# Acoustic data validity and evaluation audit

**1 November 2026.** This is a development-data audit, not a final-test evaluation or a demonstration of infestation diagnosis. The original final holdout remains locked. Numbers below are recording counts; recordings from a tree, session or source are not independent replicates.

## Data and label provenance

The local Kaggle TreeVibes v1 inventory contains 58,797 WAVs: 4,154 field and 54,643 laboratory (53,676 under lab/infested and 967 under lab/clean). The full campaign's workbook-based curation includes 3,806 field files; 348 field files with unresolved ancestry are excluded. Lab files are inventoried but not used for supervised training because parent recording relationships are not established. The earlier ML-for-Senior project is a separate research implementation and benchmark, not an independently verified extra source in this campaign.

The workbook has one AUDIO value per folder, copied to every recording from that folder. All 34 included folders are consequently single-label by construction, not evidence of a separate per-clip examination. The label is weak acoustic borer activity, not a confirmed infestation status per tree or species; folder 35 itself is described as negative recordings from infested trees and excluded for ambiguous ancestry. Workbook VISUAL and CONFIRMATION are separate fields, not substituted for verified biological ground truth. Conditions differ: folder 7/9 negatives mention orange/apricot trees and wind, folder 8 negatives fig trees and rain, whereas many positives describe urban locations. The workbook lists device IDs, coordinates and comments about drill-bit sensors/waveguides, but no verified complete tree and session identifiers or complete sensor mounting ancestry. The original [TreeVibes study](https://www.mdpi.com/2624-6511/4/1/17) describes the vibroacoustic equipment and source dataset; it does not supply the missing per-recording ancestry here.

The old manifest has 3,806 records across 34 folders, with 45 repeated decoded-PCM entries; identical PCM hashes are merged into the same split. No known component, exact PCM hash, or recorded device identifier crosses splits. The diagnostic list has 89 **unreviewed** candidate pairs; 65 connect development train and validation. Similarity is not proof of duplication, and the candidate search did not cover final-test audio. The split cannot be signed off from hashes alone. The notebook previously hashed textual `NA` coordinates as a valid site; this falsely merged unknown sites (notably folders 10 and 15). Its site overlap checks therefore do not establish known geographic ancestry for those rows. Missing site is now left unknown for future manifests. The existing locked split and its hashes are preserved; do not rewrite it in place.

## Confounding and baseline diagnostics

In the locked training split, component-000045 has 773 positive and no negative labels, component-000105 has 20 positive and no negative labels, and component-002154 has 683 negative and no positive labels. Source component therefore perfectly determines the assigned label **in that training sample**; this is a descriptive lookup, not evidence of infestation classification. The specific assignment follows from the workbook's folder labels and conservative grouping; why the underlying recordings were collected in those conditions cannot be resolved from the available metadata. A valid model cannot be demonstrated to separate biological class from source with these training groups alone.

A logistic probe using only log RMS, duration and clipping fraction predicted training component identity with 63.43% accuracy versus 52.37% majority-class accuracy in a stratified random recording split *within the same components*. This is a source-shortcut diagnostic, not independent-site validation or proof that a neural model uses a shortcut. It does not isolate background noise from sensor, gain, tree or signal differences. A component-lookup classifier has no known IDs in the validation split, so it falls back to the training majority class and predicts all positive. A simple logistic audio-summary baseline was fitted on training records only; its threshold was the training negative-score 95th percentile. It is a diagnostic, not a tuned competitor.

| Validation method | TP | FN | FP | TN | Recall | FPR | Precision | F1 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Source lookup, unseen-component majority fallback | 774 | 0 | 292 | 0 | 100% | 100% | 72.61% | 84.13% |
| Simple RMS/duration/clipping, training-only threshold | 42 | 732 | 1 | 291 | 5.43% | 0.34% | 97.67% | 10.28% |
| Existing CNN, validation-selected | 495 | 279 | 14 | 278 | 63.95% | 4.79% | 97.25% | 77.16% |
| CNN + AST blend, validation-selected | 554 | 220 | 13 | 279 | 71.58% | 4.45% | 97.71% | 82.62% |

Precision depends on the 72.61% positive prevalence in this validation set and should not be projected to field prevalence. CNN and blend thresholds and candidates were selected after extensive reuse of this same validation data; the baseline's threshold was not. The CNN's per-folder recall ranges from 0/68 (folder 2) to 68/69 (folder 5), among others; FPR is 0/238 for folder 8, 2/27 for folder 24 and 12/27 for folder 25. These are single-class folders and have unequal sample sizes; other class-specific rates are undefined. Full folder/model confusion matrices are in `outputs/acoustic-validity-audit/validation_by_folder.csv`.

The historical ML-for-Senior documentation reports 98.17% recall and 5.56% FPR under a different dataset/split and selection protocol, with file/duplicate grouping but no verified tree/session independence. Its historical model artifacts were unavailable. It is not a reproduction, a directly comparable benchmark, or a causal explanation for the present result. The prior 72.22% multi-model blend was further selected on this same validation set and is not an independent improvement.

## Changes and remaining evidence

The training notebook now merges shared `session_id` and reviewed `duplicate_group_id` values transitively along with tree, device, site, parent and exact PCM. It also treats nonnumeric/missing workbook coordinates as unknown instead of a common site. `tests/test_acoustic_lineage.py` checks both behaviors. These are safeguards for future manifests, not a retroactive repair of missing metadata: a session ID must be supplied from evidence, and only confirmed duplicate relationships should receive a duplicate group. Exact hashes do not identify transformed, segmented or near-duplicate audio. Review candidate ancestry without inspecting holdout scores; if confirmed relations cross the lock, retire the split and create a new campaign. The corrected code changes campaign identity and must not overwrite the old lock. No final holdout was opened.

No additional acoustic model was trained: doing so cannot resolve class/source confounding or provide independent confirmation, and further model selection on the repeatedly reused validation set would add optimism. Collect independently verified infestation and species labels, tree and source IDs, date/session/parent recording, device and sensor/mounting metadata, consistent protocols and noise conditions, and multiple independent trees and sites per class with both classes represented across devices and contexts. Resolve original recording ancestry, including lab parents and all flagged candidate pairs; obtain original collection logs or contact dataset authors when records are unavailable. Design a new group-disjoint development split and a new genuinely independent locked evaluation cohort, freeze the data-processing procedure and model/threshold before evaluation, and report tree/source-level uncertainty. Where verified grouping connects most records, accept that fewer independent units exist rather than splitting linked recordings. Do not declare the existing holdout independent without evidence, and do not infer unknown IDs from proximity or timestamps alone.

Reproduce the development-only summary with `python scripts/acoustic_validity_audit.py` from the repository root after restoring the saved campaign artifacts and development dependencies. It reads saved manifest metadata and validation predictions, never test audio. Outputs: `outputs/acoustic-validity-audit/metrics.json`, `development_components.csv` and `validation_by_folder.csv`; the original run directory also holds `splits.csv`, `near_duplicate_candidates.csv`, and the incomplete review template. Existing results remain exploratory and unsuitable for deployment claims.

## Additional provenance and development diagnostics — 1 November 2026

The original authors explain that `AUDIO` is a human listener's judgment, `VISUAL` is external visual inspection, and `CONFIRMATION` describes trunk slicing to find larvae ([original study, Appendix A](https://www.mdpi.com/2624-6511/4/1/17); [open preprint](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202011.0411/v1/download)). The study describes approximately one folder per tree, several months of collection, 8 kHz audio, and field WAVs decompressed from transmitted Ogg; a folder should not automatically be equated to a verified tree ID. It reports field collection from visibly affected trees and cut mulberry trunks, and notes that many controls used other tree species. Those design choices, as well as weather and sensor placement, can confound background and class. `CONFIRMATION` is valuable provenance, but the workbook alone does not give a per-clip species identification, full inspection records or a validated interpretation of every row; its YES/NO values must not be automatically converted to independent clip labels. The paper discusses a piezoelectric probe and metal waveguide; workbook comments mention drill-bit sensors and waveguides. The exact sensor/attachment and session for each clip still need original records. This refines the previous description of confirmation as merely a separate field.

All 65 cross-split candidate pairs were compared using their hash-verified development WAVs. For every pair we recorded filename timestamps, folder, labels, source fingerprint distance, exact decoded PCM hash equality, and the maximum absolute normalized waveform cross-correlation over all integer sample lags with at least five seconds overlap. Correlations range from 0.021 to 0.185, and no pair shares an exact PCM hash. Thus no direct, offset or gain-scaled waveform duplicate was confirmed by this procedure. This does **not** rule out shorter overlaps, filtering, speed changes, lossy re-encoding, shared recording ancestry or undetected near-duplicates. The original candidates remain UNREVIEWED, not cleared; timestamp differences are clues, not proof of unrelated sessions. Detailed evidence: `outputs/acoustic-validity-audit/cross_split_pair_evidence.csv`, reproduced by `python scripts/review_audio_pairs.py`. Only train/validation audio was accessed.

A fixed-seed subset of up to five recordings per validation folder (125 recordings total; 110 positive and 15 negative) was tested with the previously frozen CNN and threshold. Independent white Gaussian noise was added per one-second window at 10 dB signal-to-noise ratio, using window RMS; this is a deliberately synthetic perturbation, not a recording of traffic, rain or other field noise. Recomputed clean scores matched saved scores within 2e-5. On the clean subset the confusion matrix was TN=12, FP=3, FN=41, TP=69; after noise it was TN=15, FP=0, FN=106, TP=4. Predictions changed for 68/125 clips. This demonstrates sensitivity under this perturbation, not an unbiased estimate of natural-environment performance and not proof that noise caused the original validation results. No threshold or model was tuned on this test. Detailed per-record results: `outputs/acoustic-validity-audit/noise_sensitivity.csv`; GPU-capable reproduction: `python scripts/acoustic_noise_sensitivity.py` with the CUDA PyTorch directory on `PYTHONPATH`.

Reorganization with existing evidence remains insufficient: under the original conservative development grouping there is only one negative training component, so holding that component out leaves no negative training examples. Holding out either positive-only component permits only a positive-class diagnostic and no independent false-positive estimate. The larger mixed validation component can provide descriptive within-component contrasts, but it does not make the training negatives independent of source and is already heavily reused. Excluding the 65 candidates without establishing ancestry would neither validate remaining links nor resolve the class/source confounding. The corrected missing-site parsing can split an artificial unknown-site union in a **new** manifest, but unknown locations and sessions must not be asserted independent on that basis. No defensible final evaluation or new training campaign can be justified from these records alone.

A practical minimum first collection is a **pilot**, not a statistically sufficient performance guarantee: independently verify infestation status and, for an RPW claim, species using inspection/expert evidence; record at least 10 positive and 10 negative distinct target-host trees across at least three sites, with both classes at each site where feasible, multiple sessions per tree, and devices/sensor mountings balanced across classes. Record clean and naturally noisy conditions, weather, tree/site/session/parent IDs, timestamps, device, sensor and gain; include quiet infested-tree clips and negative/uninfested controls, with uncertain labels marked unknown. Keep whole trees and linked parents together, reserve entire independent sites for a future test, and increase sample sizes based on the desired uncertainty and observed number of independent trees (not clip count). At a 5% target FPR, even zero false positives in 10 independent negative trees cannot establish a 5% upper bound: roughly 59 independent negative units with zero errors are needed for a one-sided 95% bound near 5%, under an independent Bernoulli assumption. Actual design should account for site clustering. Acquire original collection/inspection logs or dataset-author clarification to resolve the old pair list and ancestry; the project cannot manufacture these records.

Of the 65 reviewed cross-split candidates, 28 have opposite workbook labels. That does not establish either leakage or a label conflict without confirmation of common audio ancestry. Numeric coordinates outside latitude ±90° or longitude ±180° are now treated as unknown, just like missing coordinates; this validation does not infer or repair actual locations.

## Why ML-for-Senior reports ~98% recall — split-leakage diagnostic (1 November 2026)

`outputs/reference-ml-for-senior/treevibes/data.py` assigns each file its own group (`grouping='file_and_duplicate_only'`) unless an explicit group CSV is supplied, so its split is effectively a random recording-level split; its own README says this does not prove generalization to unseen trees. `scripts/split_leakage_demo.py` compares, on the 2,542 development (train+val) recordings only, a fixed MFCC-summary random forest under two 5-fold schemes: random recording-level folds gave **100.00% recall / 1.85% FPR**, whereas folds holding out whole workbook folders (30 folders) gave **65.79% recall / 62.56% FPR**. A trivial model reaching near-perfect scores under recording-level splitting shows the high figure reflects neighbouring recordings from the same folder/tree/session (and class-source confounding) rather than detection ability. Folder is only a proxy for tree/session. This is a diagnostic, not a tuned result; the final holdout was not read.

## Local GPU three-track campaign, folder-grouped CV (1 November 2026)

`scripts/grouped_cv_campaign.py` (run with `--smoke` first; GPU-only, RTX 2070, torch 2.5.1+cu121) scores the three requested tracks on the 2,542 development recordings (1,567 positive / 975 negative, 53,176 one-second windows, 30 folders) with 5-fold GroupKFold that holds out whole workbook folders. The test partition is never read. Features reuse the notebook's exact `mfcc-8k-30x13-v1` MFCC and 59×64 log-mel code; window scores are averaged per recording. Neural models were trained with 3 seeds each (12 epochs, AdamW, pos-weighted BCE, training-only noise augmentation, training-fold normalisation). Recall@FPR thresholds come from pooled out-of-fold negatives (mildly optimistic). Outputs: `outputs/grouped-cv-campaign/full/` (`results.json`, `oof_predictions.csv`, `run_info.json`).

| Track | Model | Params | ROC-AUC | Recall @5% FPR (seeds) |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| A unrestricted | log-mel 59×64 CNN w32 | 101,441 | 0.90–0.96 | 45.1%, 74.9%, 57.3% (3-seed ensemble 57.1%) |
| A unrestricted | AST embedding + logistic | 769 head | 0.56 | 8.7% |
| B 30×13 MFCC | MFCC CNN w32 | 101,441 | 0.35–0.65 | 10.8%, 2.7%, 14.3% |
| B/C | MFCC 52 stats + logistic | 53 | 0.55 | 19.5% |
| C ESP32 candidate | log-mel CNN w8 | 6,545 | 0.91–0.95 | 55.7%, 47.8%, 39.3% |
| C ESP32 candidate | log-mel CNN w4 | 1,705 | 0.85–0.92 | 49.2%, 33.6%, 58.0% |
| C | MFCC tiny CNN w4 | 441 | 0.21–0.48 | ~1% |

**Interpretation.** The log-mel CNNs appear strong, but the effect is driven by three negative folders (7, 9: orange/apricot trees with wind; 8: fig trees with rain) that the model scores low. The only negatives recorded by the same devices as many positives (folders 24 and 25) score 0.93–0.99, i.e. they are called infested; within device for folder 24, AUC is 0.29–0.42 (below chance). The development data contain just five negative folders, so this is consistent with the model learning host-tree/environment rather than borer activity, and cannot be called detection. Seed-to-seed recall at 5% FPR varies by up to 30 percentage points, and a single earlier run of the same w32 model gave 68.9% then 93.0%: single-run figures are unreliable. The 30×13 MFCC track is near chance under folder hold-out. A 6.5k-parameter log-mel CNN (≈6.5 KB int8 weights, estimate) matches the 101k model, so model size is not the ESP32 bottleneck; flash/RAM/latency/energy remain unmeasured on WROOM32. No model or threshold was frozen, and the final holdout was not evaluated. Achieving genuinely high, trustworthy recall needs negative recordings from the same palm hosts, sites and devices as the positives.

### Whole-recording inputs and uncapped operating points (1 November 2026)

`scripts/grouped_cv_fullclip.py` (same folds, development data only) adds whole-recording inputs. Track B now uses one 30×13 MFCC matrix per ~20 s recording (notebook MFCC spec, frames split into 30 equal chunks, mean- or max-pooled, as in ML-for-Senior's `np.array_split`); tracks A/C use the full 640×64 log-mel (20.48 s). Training adds SpecAugment, mixup, OneCycle and 40 epochs (3 seeds). `scripts/grouped_cv_round2.py` holds the shared trainer and the uncapped metrics. The "balanced point" is the threshold maximising recall − FPR on pooled out-of-fold scores, so it is optimistic. Outputs: `outputs/grouped-cv-campaign/fullclip/`.

| A | 1 s log-mel CNN w32 (round 1) | 3×101k | 0.95 | 86.1% / 7.9% / 88.4% / 0.90 | 0.98 / 0.94 |

| C | **1 s log-mel CNN w8 (round 1)** | 3×6.5k | 0.95 | **97.5% / 13.5% / 93.3% / 0.95** | 0.99 / 0.95 |

Whole-clip pooling is a real improvement for the 30×13 MFCC track (AUC 0.52 → 0.76); full-clip context did not beat 1 s windows for log-mel. Per-window channel normalisation (CMVN) consistently hurt, meaning stationary spectral colour carries much of the predictive information, consistent with recording-condition confounding (it cannot distinguish that from genuine host-tissue acoustics). In every strong model the same-device negative folders 24 (and usually 25) are scored as infested, so the headline numbers reflect separation from the five available negative folders, not verified detection. All figures are development cross-validation, not final-test results; no model or threshold is frozen.
